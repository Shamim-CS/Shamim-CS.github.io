Research

Intrusion detection that learns from its mistakes

Detectors usually forget why they failed. My thesis keeps that knowledge: agents analyze each error, write it down as a readable rule, and retrieve it for new traffic. I am extending this to small local models and to federated settings where sites share rules instead of data.

IEEE SMC 2026, M.S. thesis

MA-IDS: a multi-agent RAG framework that learns from its errors

LLMs reading raw network-flow numbers tend to repeat the same mistakes. In MA-IDS, a Traffic Classification Agent labels each flow and an Error Analysis Agent turns every misclassification into a human-readable rule, stored in a FAISS experience library. At test time the library is frozen and the best-matching rules guide the LLM.

Macro F1 of 89.75% on NF-BoT-IoT and 85.22% on NF-ToN-IoT, compared with 17% and 4.96% for the same model zero-shot.

SERA-IDS: structured experience for small, local language models

Free-text rules leave the model to guess the numeric boundaries between attacks. SERA-IDS writes structured rules with explicit feature conditions, class confusions, confidence and provenance, admits them through a confidence gate, and matches their conditions to each new flow before a small model decides.

With no fine-tuning and no paid API, three local models (Llama 3.1, Phi-4 14B, Qwen2.5 7B) go from under 12% macro F1 to between 69.5% and 87.7%.

IEEE WIECON-ECE 2026

BaT-SAM: automatic brain tumor segmentation with SAM

The Segment Anything Model needs a user prompt and lacks the local bias that MRI segmentation benefits from. BaT-SAM adds Conv-LoRA adapters with a mixture of convolutional experts to the frozen encoder, training under 1% of the parameters, and a self-prompting box regressor so no prompt is needed.

Weighted mean IoU of 0.832 on BRISC2025, and Dice of 0.784 on LGG MRI and 0.702 on Figshare without retraining.

IEEE COMPAS 2026, co-first author

Fine-tuned video transformers for harassment detection

Few labeled videos exist for detecting sexual harassment, and clothing, lighting, occlusion and camera angle all vary. We doubled the SHV dataset to 600 clips, including everyday contact that looks similar but is not harassment, and fine-tuned three video transformers pretrained on Kinetics-400.

TimeSformer reached 92.27% test accuracy, ahead of VideoMAE (91.71%) and ViViT (91.16%).
